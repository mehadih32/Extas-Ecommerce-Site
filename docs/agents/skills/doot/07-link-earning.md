## 1. The line we never cross (Google spam policy) [OFFICIAL]

Link spam includes:
- buying or selling links for ranking — "Exchanging goods or services for links", including "Sending someone a product in exchange for them writing about it and including a link";
- "Requiring a link as part of a Terms of Service, contract, or similar arrangement";
- excessive link exchanges, automated link building, low-quality directory/bookmark links, site-wide footer links.
Paid or gifted placements are fine **only** when the links carry `rel="sponsored"` (or `nofollow`). Also banned: expired-domain abuse, site reputation abuse (renting a strong site's section), hidden links.

So: when we gift a product to a creator or blogger, the brief says **disclose the partnership and mark links `rel="sponsored"`**. The value is the audience and the brand mention, not PageRank.

## 3. Process
1. Asset first (data study, tool, guide) — no outreach without something worth linking to.
2. List 30–50 relevant BD journalists/blogs/creators (name, desk, recent article).
3. Short personalised pitch in the outlet's language (Bengali for Bangla outlets), with the key finding in the first line, a chart, and the owner as quotable expert.
4. Track: pitched → replied → covered → linked (and `rel` value).
5. Never pay for a dofollow link; never accept "guest post packages".

## 4. Measuring
- Referring domains from Search Console → Links report (free) and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (free for a verified own site) **[COMMUNITY: AWT free tier per Ahrefs tutorial]**.
- Quality > count: one Daily Star link beats 100 directory links.
- Watch for spammy inbound links; Google largely ignores them — disavow only for a manual action or a clear paid-link history **[COMMUNITY — widely reported Google stance; verify before using disavow]**.

## 5. Honest expectations
New brand: a handful of quality links in the first 3–6 months is good. Links take weeks to be crawled and counted; don't expect ranking jumps the week a link appears **[COMMUNITY]**.
