## 1. Theory you need [ACADEMIC: Stremersch & Tellis, Journal of Marketing 2002]

- **Price bundling** = separate products sold together at a discount, no integration → needs a discount to attract buyers. **Product bundling** = integrated new offering (e.g. a matched outfit designed as one look) → can justify value beyond the sum.
- **Pure** bundling (only the set), **mixed** (set and singles), **unbundling** (singles only).
- **Mixed bundling beats pure** when customers value the bundle differently; in competitive markets mixed dominates. → EXTAS: sell combos **and** singles (but the owner's "no single piece in this section" rule applies to combo sections — singles live elsewhere).
- Price bundling pays more when contribution margins are high; presentation matters: a single bundle price tends to lower price sensitivity, and **discounts are better presented as several separate savings**.
- Firms with market power should favour value-adding integration over short-term price bundling → EXTAS's colour-paired, designed combos (product bundling) are stronger than "any 3 for X" (pure price bundling).

## 2. Choice architecture for tiers [ACADEMIC: Huber, Payne & Puto, JMR 2014]
- The **decoy (attraction) effect** needs an option clearly worse on every attribute; the original authors say it's **rare in real markets** and fragile (needs quick, visible dominance; weak prior preferences).
- The **compromise effect** (people pick the middle option) is "more often seen in the marketplace" (same authors).
→ EXTAS: build 3-tier ladders (2 pcs · 3 pcs · 4–5 pcs) where the middle tier is the one we want most; don't rely on fake decoys; never create a dominated option just to manipulate.

## 3. EXTAS combo playbook [HOUSE + above evidence]

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Designed sets first | colour-paired outfits (auto colour-pair engine PR 571) — a look, not a pile |
| Show the saving as taka and per piece | "আলাদা কিনলে ৳2,350 · কম্বোতে ৳1,999 · প্রতি পিস ৳666" |
| Three tiers | e.g. 2 tees ৳999 · 3 tees ৳1,399 · 5 tees ৳2,199 — middle tier highlighted |
| Mix & match inside a set | let the customer choose colours/sizes per piece (mixed bundling value) |
| Delivery maths | one delivery charge for the set; free-delivery threshold set slightly above the typical single-item order (verify with data) |
| Never leave the combo | owner rule: tapping a piece doesn't jump to a single PDP |
| Stock rule | a combo is only sellable when all pieces have the chosen sizes; auto-hide or offer substitutions |
| Better-of-two offers | set price vs other offers: customer gets the better of the two, never stacked by surprise (already built) |
| Seasonal sets | winter (hoodie + jogger), Eid (panjabi + pajama/polo), office (shirt + trouser) |

## 4. AOV levers ranked for EXTAS
1. Combos/tiers (above).
2. **Free-delivery threshold** shown near Buy and in the bag with "আর ৳X কিনলে ডেলিভারি ফ্রি" (Baymard: threshold info near the Buy section, updated dynamically) [RESEARCH].
3. Complete-the-look suggestions on PDP/bag (same colour family, other category).
4. Size-confidence (fewer split orders "2 sizes to be safe" → but the 2-size rule handles that).
Avoid: pre-ticked add-ons, fake scarcity, inflated "was" prices (illegal/deceptive and against EXTAS rules).

## 5. Measuring bundles
- Combo attach rate (orders with ≥ 1 combo ÷ orders), AOV, items/order, **margin per order** (not just AOV), delivered rate of combo orders vs singles, combo-driven refusals (wrong size in a set?).
- Test: tier prices and saving framing (taka vs %) with the owner's approval per price.
