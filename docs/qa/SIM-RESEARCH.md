## 2. The customer journey, stage by stage, with real-life events

Frequencies are rough and sourced where possible; [K] items are what Bangladeshi sellers commonly report and the
simulation must prove or disprove.

### 2.1 Need and discovery
- The trigger is usually an occasion (Eid, Puja, Pohela Boishakh, office joining, wedding, winter) or a worn-out item.
  Eid alone is 35–40% of the year's clothing sales (N22).
- Discovery is mostly **Facebook/Instagram feed ads and pages**; ~80% of online lifestyle sales come from F-commerce
  (N23). Google search grows for brand names and specific items ("polo shirt price in bd"). Word of mouth and
  "who made your shirt?" in the office are strong for repeat brands [K].
- Event: the customer sees an ad, taps, lands on a landing page or product page — or **comments "price?"** under the
  post and expects a Messenger reply [K]. Pages that answer in minutes win.

### 2.2 Landing and browse
- 85% of traffic is mobile (N37); mobile abandonment ~80% (N35). Slow 3G/4G outside Dhaka makes page weight a sales
  issue, not a technical one.
- Trust is the first question (N25, N35 "lack of trust 19%"). The visitor scans for: real photos, price shown openly
  (no "inbox for price"), COD, delivery charge, exchange policy, a phone/WhatsApp, reviews.

### 2.3 Product page: size and trust
- Size anxiety is the No. 1 return reason in apparel globally (N30, N31). In BD, sizes differ by brand and fit (slim vs
  regular), so customers ask "I'm 5'8", 70 kg, which size?" on Messenger [K].
- Colour accuracy: "not delivered as ordered" is 50.7% of complaints (N4); colour mismatch stories are common (N26).
- Fabric/GSM and "will it shrink or fade?" questions; "is the photo real?" [K].

### 2.4 Pre-sale chat
- Questions arrive on Messenger and WhatsApp: price, size, delivery charge to their district, "how many days",
  "can I check before paying?", "is COD available?" [K]. A slow reply loses the sale; the AI agent must answer from the
  live rules (register PR 432) and hand over to a human when unsure.

### 2.5 Bag, offers and checkout
- Unexpected costs are the top abandonment reason (39%, N35) — so the delivery charge must show early and be right.
- Offers in BD menswear: combos ("any 2 polos ৳X"), free delivery over a threshold, Eid discounts, bKash/Nagad
  cashback during campaigns (N22).
- Phone numbers are typed in many shapes (+880, 880, Bengali digits) — the existing lesson `phoneNormalizeBD`.
- Address is free text; area names collide (Gabtoli Dhaka vs Bogura) — already engineered (PR 549–553).

### 2.6 Confirmation and fraud check
- Most sellers phone every COD order; two calls (after order, before dispatch) is a known best practice (N28).
  A confirmation call cuts RTO 15–25% (N10).
- Fake/prank orders: orders placed for a friend as a joke, competitors, children, people who order from several
  pages and accept the first to arrive [K]. Paperfly's data says most failed deliveries are **indecision or
  unreachable** (21 of 24 points, N6) — so the real enemy is low commitment, not only fraud.
- Courier success-ratio lookups by phone are common practice (N29); merchant thresholds decide whether COD stays.

### 2.7 Packing and handover
- Multi-item orders need a picking list, a checked invoice and a courier sticker with the COD amount (PR 179–182).
- Mistakes: wrong size/colour picked, a missing piece in a set, a COD amount that does not match the order after an
  edit, wrong phone on the sticker [K].

### 2.8 Courier, tracking and door delivery
- Delivery: Dhaka 1–2 days, divisional cities 3–5, rural 5–7 (N19); couriers "often take over a week" in complaints
  (N27). Eid peak stretches all of this [K].
- Door events (Steadfast terms recognise several, N16):
  - **Partial acceptance** — the customer keeps 2 of 3 items; this needs the merchant's approval at Steadfast; at
    Paperfly it costs +50% of the delivery charge (N17).
  - **Refusal at the door** — "I didn't order", "I ordered from another page already", "don't have cash now",
    "the colour looks different" [K].
  - **Try-on in front of the rider** — the owner has allowed this (register owner answers 19 Oct: "trial at delivery
    allowed, setting on").
  - **Price negotiation at the door** — the customer asks the rider for a discount or refuses to pay the delivery
    charge [K].
  - **Rider asks for extra money** ("delivery charge is more", "tips") or calls the customer to collect from a hub [K].
  - Customer unreachable / phone off / wrong number; rider marks "customer not available" without visiting [K].
  - A delivery-charge-only collection on refusal is marked "Delivered" by the courier — already handled by PR 525.
- Lost and damaged parcels: compensation is negotiated (lost) or **up to 50% of declared value** (damaged) at
  Steadfast (N16); couriers "rarely accept responsibility" in complaints (N27).

### 2.9 Money: COD settlement
- Settlement 2–5 days normally (N13), slower outside Dhaka, faster with InstaPay (N14). Deductions: delivery charge,
  1% COD charge (N16, N17), return charges where they apply. Mismatches between courier statements and orders are
  common enough that reconciliation tools exist (N8) [K for frequency].

### 2.10 Day 1–3 after delivery
- Size exchange requests (fit is the top reason, N30); colour "looks different" and quality complaints (N4: 44.8% poor
  quality); wrong item packed.
- Angry customers post publicly on Facebook (N25); being ignored or blocked is what turns a complaint into a viral post
  (N26). Speed and a human tone defuse it [K].
- Reviews: most customers do not review unless asked; reviews with photos convert best (N36).

### 2.11 Returns, exchange logistics and QC
- Exchanges travel as a new delivery + a pickup; Paperfly charges +50% for exchange (N17).
- Returned items come back unworn (resellable), slightly used / missing tags (B-grade), or damaged. Without a QC step,
  stock counts drift.

### 2.12 Repeat purchase
- Repeat buyers cost no ad money and refuse far less [K]. Product-life reminders (T-shirts ~6–9 months, polos ~9–12,
  panjabi each Eid) and occasion reminders (Eid, winter) bring them back.

## 4. What the best brands do (verify before quoting)

| Practice | Who (known examples) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Show price, delivery charge and COD openly on every product | Yellow, Aarong, Easy, Sailor, Lubnan online stores | [K] Standard among the large brands; "inbox for price" is an F-commerce habit that erodes trust |
| **Exchange at any outlet** for online orders | Aarong, Yellow, Sailor, Easy (outlet networks) | [K] Omnichannel exchange removes the exchange-shipping cost and the risk; EXTAS has no outlet yet, so the equivalent is a **pickup-exchange on the next delivery** |
| Clear size charts with garment measurements | Most large brands | [K] Few offer a height/weight recommender — EXTAS's Find My Size (PR 58–59) is a lead |
| Eid collections launched weeks early, online cut-off dates announced | Yellow (Eid online orders > 2× estimate, N23) | Eid cut-off and "order by X for delivery before Eid" is the brand standard |
| bKash/Nagad campaign tie-ins at Eid | Many brands (N22) | Bank/wallet-funded cashback lowers the brand's cost of a prepaid nudge |
| Fast pre-sale answers on Messenger, a named support number | Lubnan, Easy (F-commerce heavy) | [K] |
| Seasonal and festive landing pages reused each year | Large brands | Already PR 246, 530 |

**Global best practice that applies**

- **Post-purchase as a channel:** a branded tracking page with the delivery promise, a delivery-day reminder (N34),
  then a care message and a review request after delivery; review with photo first (N36).
- **Size recommendation and fit notes** cut fit returns; >50% of apparel returns are fit (N30).
- **Exchange-first, not refund-first:** keeps the revenue; credit to wallet when the new item is cheaper (already the
  EXTAS rule).
- **Prepaid incentives** sized below the cost of one RTO; partial advance only for risky orders (N9, N33).
- **Measure delivered revenue, not orders** — optimise ads on delivered purchases (already PR 258, 532).

## 5. A first RTO-cost model for EXTAS (to be replaced by real data)

Assumptions (owner fills the real figures in settings; nothing here is a price decision):

| Item | Low | High | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Forward delivery charge paid to the courier | ৳60 | ৳130 | N17, N18 |
| Return leg | ৳0 | ৳65 | Steadfast/Paperfly no return charge (N16, N17); others charge |
| Packaging (poly mailer, invoice, sticker, thank-you card) | ৳15 | ৳40 | [K] |
| Call and handling time (confirmation + receiving + QC) | ৳20 | ৳40 | [K] |
| Damage / B-grade write-off share | ৳30 | ৳80 | 5–10% of returns, N32 |
| Ad cost of the lost order (cost per order) | ৳120 | ৳350 | N32 scaled to BD [K] |
| **Total per RTO** | **≈৳250** | **≈৳700** | |

At 1,000 COD orders a month, every 10 points of RTO is ≈৳25,000–70,000 a month. A ৳20–30 prepaid discount
therefore pays for itself if it converts even a small share of orders that would otherwise have failed — the owner
decides the amount (hard rule 6).

## 6. What this research changes in the plan

1. **Commitment beats suspicion.** 21 of 24 failed-delivery points are indecision or unreachability (N6). The plan
   therefore tests confirmation, delivery-day reminders and the delivery promise as hard as it tests the fraud engine.
2. **Size is the biggest controllable return driver** (N30, N31) — every size-related event in the script checks the
   Find My Size tool, the fit notes and the self-service exchange.
3. **"Not as ordered" is the top complaint** (N4) — the packing checkpoint has a scan/verify step and colour-true
   photos are part of the product-page check.
4. **Partial delivery and try-on are real, contractually recognised events** (N16, N17) — the system must record
   them with the right money, stock and courier-charge effect.
5. **Eid is a third of the year** (N22) — the script includes an Eid-peak block with cut-offs and delivery promises.
6. **Cash flow matters** (N13, N14) — COD reconciliation is a back-office check, and instant settlement is a proposal.
